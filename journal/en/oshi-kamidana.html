A Private Place for Gratitude to Your Oshi—Away from Social Media

Published 2026-01-31 · Updated 2026-08-09 · Kamidana App Editorial Team

After you have prepared, supported, or shown up for someone you care about, the outcome may still be beyond your control. Instead of refreshing social media, you can place gratitude or a wish somewhere private.

Kamidana App does not define a person as a deity or promise that a wish will come true. It is a Shinto-inspired app for placing meaningful images and words, marking a pause, and returning to daily life.

A place that does not ask for a reaction

Posting on social media immediately invites comparison and response. Kamidana App has no public feed or likes. You can first acknowledge what matters to you without turning it into a performance.

How the current features differ

  • Images: Place a meaningful photo or talisman image in your kamidana. Use only images you have permission to use.
  • Journal: Keep events and gratitude you may want to revisit.
  • Ema: Write a wish alongside the next action that remains yours to take.
  • Release: Write words you do not need to keep, then mark a boundary. The screen states that this entry disappears after one day.

Return to what is yours to do

You cannot decide a ticket result or another person’s future. You can prepare, communicate, and protect your own routine. After placing the wish, close the app and return to that next action. This sequence—not a promised outcome—is the intended value.
