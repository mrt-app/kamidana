Cherishing Present Bonds, Welcoming New Encounters: The Meaning of Enmusubi

Published 2026-10-19 · Updated 2026-10-31 · Kamidana App Editorial Team

"I want to meet someone with whom I can build a sincere, mutual partnership."
"I hope my partner and I can navigate our challenges and grow closer together."
"I want my family and children to be surrounded by kind, trustworthy people."

Human life is fundamentally relational. In moments of excitement when meeting someone new, or during quiet hours of feeling alone, we may find ourselves wishing to be warmly connected with others.

At the same time, meaningful bonds already surround us in the present. We rely on the quiet support of family, the understanding of trusted friends, and the cooperation of colleagues who show up alongside us every day.

Wishing for new, fulfilling encounters while cherishing the relationships we already hold are two expressions of the same heart. In Japanese tradition, this is reflected in the practice of enmusubi—often translated as "matchmaking," but encompassing a much wider, richer understanding of human connection.

By looking at the spiritual roots of enmusubi at renowned shrines, we can learn how to cultivate relationships with openness, respect, and quiet intention.

Understanding the Traditions of Enmusubi

Throughout Japan, thousands of visitors write their wishes on wooden votive tablets (ema) or participate in ceremonies seeking auspicious ties.

However, the meaning of enmusubi is not a monolithic dogma. It has been shaped by the unique mythology and history of individual sanctuaries. Two prominent shrines offer valuable insight into how the tying of bonds has been understood.

Izumo Taisha: Tying Unseen Bonds of "Ko-En" and Mutual Flourishing

Situated in Shimane Prefecture, Izumo Taisha is widely regarded as the spiritual home of enmusubi.

The shrine is dedicated to Okuninushi-no-Okami, a revered deity known in Japanese myth for building the land and establishing agriculture, medicine, and social order. Following the legendary transfer of worldly governance (kuni-yuzuri), Okuninushi was entrusted with the unseen realm (kakuriyo or kakurigoto)—the invisible world of human destiny, spirit, and relationships.

According to Izumo Taisha's traditions, enmusubi is far broader than romance alone. It refers to ko-en (幸縁)—auspicious connections that enable all living beings to harmonize, prosper, and bring out the best in one another. According to long-standing lore, during the tenth month of the lunar calendar (Kamiarizuki, or the "Month When Deities Gather"), deities from across Japan assemble at Izumo for kamuhakari (sacred deliberation) to consult on the relationships and fortunes of human lives. This custom reflects deep reverence for the mysterious ways people are brought together across time and circumstance.

Tokyo Daijingu: The Creative Force of Musubi and Modern Weddings

In central Tokyo, Tokyo Daijingu stands as another revered center for relationship prayers. Established in 1880 during the Meiji era as a central pavilion for worshipping the deities of Ise Grand Shrine, it is known affectionately as the "Ise Shrine of Tokyo."

In addition to the sun deity Amaterasu and the harvest deity Toyouke, Tokyo Daijingu enshrines the Zoka-no-Sanshin—the three primordial deities of creation described in the earliest records of Japan: Amenominakanushi, Takamimusuhi, and Kamimusuhi.

In classical Japanese thought, the word "musubi" denotes the generative, creative vitality that causes things to come into being, grow, and intertwine. Because these creator deities embody the fundamental power of connection, the shrine became a beloved sanctuary for seeking auspicious unions. Furthermore, in 1900, Tokyo Daijingu established Japan’s first public Shinto wedding ceremonies, creating a lasting legacy that links formal relationship vows to sacred tradition.

Nurturing Connections with Openness and Respect

When we pray for love or interpersonal harmony, keeping an open, caring heart is essential.

Wishing someone well is an expression of genuine warmth. Because true connection blossoms naturally when both individuals feel valued, gently honoring another person's pace and autonomy forms the true foundation of lasting bonds.

Honoring Others and Cultivating a Calm Heart

When we care deeply about someone, it is natural to look forward to their words and affection.

At the same time, each person travels their own journey and carries their own daily realities. Bringing a relationship before a quiet altar offers a moment to reaffirm our genuine goodwill, steady our emotions, and nurture deeper empathy for those around us.

Trusting Encounters, Offering Gentle Everyday Kindness

There is a quiet wonder in how people cross paths in life. Entrusting those mysterious timings to sacred grace allows us to focus on the warm, sincere ways we can relate to others today:

  • If you are seeking new connections: Present yourself with care and cleanliness, step into communities that align with your genuine interests, and welcome others with an open mind.
  • If you are deepening an existing bond: Listen patiently without rushing, express heartfelt appreciation for small everyday courtesies, and honor your commitments.

Offering sincere greetings, kindness, and patience creates an environment where mutual trust can flourish. When we cultivate a gracious presence, healthy relationships find natural ground to grow.
